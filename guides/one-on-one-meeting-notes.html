How to run and document a 1-on-1 meeting

A good 1-on-1 has a light agenda, mostly listening, and written notes that end with clear action items, each with an owner and a date. Open the next meeting by reviewing those items.

By Karuppasamy Pandiyan Marimuthu · Last updated 2026-09-24

Run a 1-on-1 in 5 steps

  1. Before the meeting, read your last notes and any open follow-ups.
  2. Agree a short agenda: the other person's topics first, then yours.
  3. Listen more than you talk, and ask open questions.
  4. Write down decisions and action items, each with an owner and a due date.
  5. Start the next meeting by checking whether those follow-ups are done.

What to write down

  • The date and who attended.
  • Key takeaways in two or three lines.
  • Decisions made and the reason for them.
  • Follow-ups with owner and date.
  • Personal context worth remembering, such as goals or concerns the person shared.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping meetings when things are busy, which is when they matter most.
  • Taking notes but never reviewing them.
  • Recording tasks without an owner or a date.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a 1-on-1 be?

Many managers use 30 minutes weekly or every two weeks. The right length is the one you will keep consistently.
